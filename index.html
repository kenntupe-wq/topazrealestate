Valley-Wide · June 2026

SALT LAKE COUNTY COOLS INTO A BUYER-FRIENDLIER SUMMER

After the breakneck appreciation of 2021–2022, the Salt Lake Valley market has settled into something closer to balance. The median sold price across Salt Lake County is now around $561,000, still up modestly year over year, while the median listing price has actually edged down slightly — a small but telling gap that favors buyers over sellers for the first time in years.

Active listings have climbed to roughly 4,700, up about 4% from last year, giving buyers more to choose from than they've had in some time. Homes are also sitting on the market longer — around 43 days on average — which means fewer rushed decisions and waived contingencies, and more room to negotiate repairs, closing costs, or a rate buydown.

Mortgage rates have settled near 6.1% on a 30-year fixed as of mid-June, which is workable for most qualified buyers but still sensitive to any move back toward 7%. Overall, this looks less like a slowdown and more like a market catching its breath after years of overheating.

City-by-City · June 2026

WHERE PRICES ARE CLIMBING FASTEST ACROSS THE VALLEY

Pricing trends vary sharply once you zoom into individual cities. Sandy posted the largest year-over-year jump in the county, with its median climbing to roughly $755,000 — a gain north of 16%. Salt Lake City followed with a more moderate increase, landing near $580,000.

West Valley City and West Jordan saw steadier, single-digit appreciation, with medians around $480,000 and $560,000 respectively. West Valley City remains the most affordable of the four major submarkets, while Sandy and West Jordan continue to carry the highest price points in the valley.

The takeaway for buyers: location-specific research matters more than ever. A "cooling" headline at the county level can mask real heat in individual neighborhoods, and vice versa.

Rates & Inventory · June 2026

THE "RATE LOCK-IN EFFECT" IS STILL HOLDING BACK INVENTORY

One of the biggest forces shaping today's market isn't demand — it's supply. More than 60% of Utah homeowners with a mortgage are locked into rates below 4%, and many are choosing to stay put rather than trade that rate away for something closer to 6%.

That restraint is a major reason single-family inventory hasn't ballooned even as buyer demand has softened. Meanwhile, Utah's economy continues adding jobs — on the order of 27,000 new positions this year — but net migration into the state has slowed as housing costs have risen relative to other markets.

For sellers, this means less competition than headlines might suggest. For buyers, it means inventory gains are likely to stay gradual rather than dramatic for the rest of 2026.

2026 Forecast · June 2026

WHAT IT ACTUALLY TAKES TO QUALIFY FOR THE MEDIAN HOME NOW

Local housing economists estimate that buying the median-priced home in Salt Lake County now calls for a household income near $150,000 — typically requiring two incomes and several years of career growth to reach. That affordability squeeze is reshaping who's buying and what they're buying.

Builders are adjusting by shrinking floor plans. Roughly half of today's buyers are single, and demand has shifted toward smaller, lower-maintenance homes rather than the larger family-sized lots that dominated past years.

Looking ahead, county forecasters expect prices to keep rising at a modest, sustainable pace rather than spike or crash — good news for anyone hoping the market will hand them a discount, but also a sign that waiting on the sidelines won't pay off the way some buyers hope.
